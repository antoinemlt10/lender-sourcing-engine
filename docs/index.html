Work sample for Kita, Chief of Staff, Founders' Office. Antoine Mouillet, 18 September 2026.

142 Philippine lenders, taken from the SEC and BSP registers, scored against a hypothesis of who buys Kita, and checked for how much the ranking is worth

A tool, not a memo. The code, the config and the ranked list are in a public repository. Every lender comes from a regulator's list with its certificate number and the list's date. Every score has a rationale and evidence marked sourced or inferred. And the tool tests its own output before anyone trusts it.

What I did. I took a sourcing engine I had built for an earlier application, replaced its hand-typed seed list with the SEC lists of lending, financing and online-lending companies and the BSP directory of banks, rewrote the scoring rubric for lenders, and ran it on the Philippines. It read 6,605 register rows, kept 174 in an enrichment pool (digital banks, thrift banks, operators of a recorded online lending platform), and enriched 142 of them from public web data with Claude and web search, two calls per lender, for a measured $58 of API. Then it ran distribution checks on its own ranking: ties, factors that move no one, share of evidence with a URL behind it, and how much of the top 15 survives a 0.10 shift in any weight.

What it shows. That I can go from a market I do not know to a list that can be checked line by line in two working days, saying on each line what is known and what is guessed. And that I test a ranking before I trust it: on Skophos, an AI product I built and shipped solo in six weeks this year, I found 160 papers tied at the same score, which made the top 25 random, and rebuilt the scorer. The checks below are the ones I wish I had run first.

What it does not show. Anything about Philippine lending law or credit risk in emerging markets. I know neither; the rubric is my reading of who buys Kita, from public material only, and it is the part of this page I most expect you to correct. Nothing here has been checked against your pipeline. Eleven accounts sit at zero because of a rubric defect I found and documented rather than paid to fix, and 32 of the 174 seeds were never enriched because I stopped the run on budget. Both are listed below.

Where the pool comes from

registers, as of the date printed on each

No lender was typed in. The manifest in the repository names each list, its URL and its date; the parser writes a reviewable extract per list, and every seed keeps its register row. The SEC's own menu still links lists dated 31 May 2020; the most recent full lists of lending and financing companies on sec.gov.ph are PDFs dated 30 September 2023, found by search. They feed the full pool only. The online lending platform list and the BSP directory are current.

The ranking

top 15 of 142; full list in the repository

Seven factors, each 0 to 1, each with a rationale and cited evidence. Closeability is a gate: below 0.5 the account scores 0 and is listed as ineligible. The other three structural factors combine as a geometric mean, because each one can kill an account on its own. The operational factors add. The floor keeps a structurally strong lender visible in a quiet quarter.

eligible = closeability >= 0.5 (else final = 0) structural = (acuity * roi_quant * whitespace) ^ (1/3) operational = 0.35 * winnability + 0.35 * active_pain_timing + 0.30 * reachability final = round(100 * structural * (0.4 + 0.6 * operational))

What the ranking is worth

outputs/diagnostics.md, generated by the tool

Ties

39 distinct scores for 142 accounts; 133 share a score with at least one other. The largest groups: 10 accounts at 28, 8 accounts at 25, 7 accounts at 24, and the 11 at zero. The top-15 cut does not fall inside a tie: rank 15 scores 40, rank 16 scores 38.

Stability under a 0.10 shift

Each operational weight was moved by plus and minus 0.10 (renormalized), and the floor likewise. In all eight cases the same 15 accounts stay in the top 15; the full order correlates at 0.995 or better with the baseline. The order encodes the accounts more than the weights.

The check that moved

Before six accounts were re-enriched under a tightened prompt, the same tool reported a three-way tie at 41 straddling the top-15 cut and 13 of 15 kept under a floor shift. The rubric had not changed. Enrichment is not deterministic, and its noise is at least as large as a 0.10 weight shift. The next check to write is a repeat-enrichment test on a fixed sample.

Known defects of this run

found by me, left visible
  • Eleven accounts at zero. The first wording of the closeability rubric read a lifted revocation or cease-and-desist order, noted as lifted on the current SEC register, as disqualifying, and the gate turned that into 0. The rubric now says an order counts only while in force. Re-enriching the eleven is about $4 I did not spend; their rank would change. They are Legazpi Savings Bank, Inc., Cash Mart Asia Lending Inc., Elending Lending Inc, Fast Coin Lending Corp, Cashbee Lending Services Inc., Gofcash Lending Corporation operating under th…, Hi-Fin Lending, Inc., Kayamo Atlas Lending Corp, Lucky Shell Fintech Lending, Inc., Makati Loan, Inc. dba Peso Cash Loan, Microdot Lending Corporation. One of them, Legazpi Savings Bank, is at zero for a different reason: the BSP has approved its merger into another bank.
  • 32 seeds not enriched. The run went through the operators alphabetically and stopped on budget at 142 of 174, so the missing 32 are the tail from Scoreone Financing to Yulon Finance. That tail includes names you will look for first: Tala Financing Philippines Inc, Skyro Lending Inc, Sunprime Finance Inc, Toyota Financial Services Philippines Corporation, Sumisho Motor Finance Corporation. They are listed with their register ids in the repository, and they would cost about $12.
  • No account brief. The engine's brief command was run twice on the top-ranked lender. Both briefs attributed statements to the bank's president without a URL that supported them. I deleted both. A brief that cannot be checked sentence by sentence is worse than none.
  • Individuals named inside evidence claims. The prompts forbid personal names, and the committed files (rankings, rationales, angles) are clean and pass an automated check for emails, phone numbers and profile pages. The model still quoted a named speaker inside three accounts' raw evidence claims when the press source named them. The raw JSON is not published and this page does not render claims verbatim.
  • Old SEC lists in the full pool. The lending and financing company lists are dated 30 September 2023; I found no later version on sec.gov.ph. The enriched pool does not depend on them.

What is hypothesis, and how to change it

configs/kita_philippines_icp.json
  • The wedge. That Kita's next Philippine customers are regulated lenders whose book is thin-file credit decided from documents, with a visible manual step. Written from Kita's public launch and press, not from anything you told me.
  • The rubrics and weights. Each factor's anchors are mine. The three structural factors are equal; the operational weights are 0.35, 0.35, 0.30; the floor is 0.4. All of it is text and numbers in one JSON file. Editing it and running rank re-scores the stored accounts with no API calls; editing a rubric's text needs re-enrichment.
  • The incumbents list is empty on purpose. I do not know which loan origination or decisioning vendors are installed in Philippine lenders. Whitespace was scored on whatever the web enrichment found. Your team can fill the list in one edit.
  • Which registers to enrich. Digital, thrift and online-platform lenders were my guess at where the document pain is visible. Rural banks and the 887 financing companies may be where your pipeline actually is.
  • The test that matters. Put the top 15 next to your real closed and lost accounts. If ranks 1 to 15 do not convert to first meetings visibly better than ranks 30 to 45, the rubric is wrong. That is a config edit, and the engine is built to make the retry cheap.

What this does not do

said plainly
  • It does not know Philippine lending law or credit risk in emerging markets. It reads registers and public pages, and it marks what it could not source.
  • It has no contact data and no individuals' names in any published file. Personas are roles.
  • It has not been validated against any pipeline, and its scores are not predictions of anything until it has been.
  • It was built and run in two working days on a budget of about $60. The full list of what I would do next is in the README: prompt caching (the rubric was re-sent on every call), a repeat-enrichment test, a hard join on licence status, and your pipeline data.
